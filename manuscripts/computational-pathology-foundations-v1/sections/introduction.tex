\section{Introduction}

A pathology system can preserve tissue identity yet remain sensitive to scanner acquisition. It can also perform well on pooled slides while allowing one large site to dominate learning under a shifted label process. These are distinct mechanisms, but they pose a common question: \emph{which information is available to the model, and which source is allowed to exert influence?} Answering it requires controlled comparisons at more than one level of the pathology workflow.

Paired-Acquisition Neural Factorization (PA-NF) begins with a particularly informative intervention: the same tissue region is imaged on multiple scanners. Biological-region identity is held fixed while acquisition changes, making paired consistency observable without requiring a diagnostic label for every patch. A neural factorizer maps pretrained image features into tissue-oriented and acquisition-oriented branches, with joint reconstruction and scanner-routing objectives. The empirical claim is not that those branches are uniquely identifiable or perfectly pure. It is that the trained representation changes \emph{measured} scanner recoverability while preserving a prespecified tissue-region retrieval endpoint.

The broader research program examines two subsequent decision levels. TransnnMIL aggregates patch representations into whole-slide predictions; PathologyFL and institutional-weighting experiments ask how updates or source models should be combined when client volume and validation quality diverge. These are implemented components of one research codebase, but the SCORPION representation, PANDA whole-slide, and institutional-weighting results were obtained in \emph{separate experimental settings}. No result in this paper identifies the causal effect of carrying a learned PA-NF branch through a fully trained representation-to-slide-to-federation chain.

Three findings define the paper's evidentiary contribution:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Paired representation learning.} A registered SCORPION campaign (seven variants, five slide-blocked folds, five seeds; 175 fits) demonstrates lower linear scanner recoverability from the tissue branch than an equal-capacity neural control, with same-region retrieval noninferiority. Cross-backbone tests, an independent five-scanner canine comparison against strong linear corrections, and two exploratory ScanComp physical-slide retrieval replications bound where this advantage transfers and where it does not.
\item \textbf{Whole-slide architecture under matched controls.} The repaired TransnnMIL family and conventional MIL models were evaluated in a distinct 35-fit PANDA comparison. The primary repaired-versus-TransMIL result is negative; retaining it prevents architectural implementation from being confused with demonstrated predictive superiority.
\item \textbf{Conditional institutional influence.} Under simulated PANDA dominant-site corruption, a fixed validation-triggered detector sometimes improves on sample-proportional aggregation; a separate CAMELYON17 feature-level proxy shows that reweighting source centers can improve accuracy on an unseen center. Neither is a prospective multi-hospital deployment result.
\end{enumerate}

PCam patch training, controlled synthetic factor experiments, spatial recurrence tests, and the surrounding reproducibility infrastructure support these investigations but are not promoted as additional independent clinical confirmations. The experimental units, endpoint definitions, and comparator capabilities are kept distinct throughout.

The central thesis is narrower than universal scanner invariance or a new pathology leaderboard: \emph{paired acquisition and conditional source weighting can change measured nuisance accessibility and institutional influence without assuming that all useful task information should be removed or that sample count alone determines authority}. Each part of this thesis is tested only against the controls and endpoints available in its corresponding experiment.
