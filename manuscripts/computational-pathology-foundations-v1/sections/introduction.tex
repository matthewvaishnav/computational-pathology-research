\section{Introduction}

Computational-pathology neural systems aggregate information across several
distinct levels. At the first level, image patches are transformed into
representations, either by a frozen foundation model or by a task-trained
encoder. At the second level, patch instances are aggregated into whole-slide
predictions through multiple-instance learning. At the third level,
institutional model updates are aggregated into collaborative models through
federated learning. These levels are usually studied as separate engineering
problems, yet the artifacts that threaten them are shared: scanner and stain
variation, acquisition workflow differences, site and center identity, label
noise, optimization failures, and institutional imbalance can all be preserved,
shortcut-exploited, or amplified as information passes from one aggregation
stage to the next.

This research program treats those aggregation stages as a connected system and
develops the neural architectures, paired-data designs,
institutional-weighting protocols, stress tests, and provenance controls needed
to build and audit them. The program spans:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Representation formation.} Patch and foundation-model
  representations and the scanner, stain, acquisition, site, region, and
  dataset artifacts they encode, studied through exact same-region paired
  scanner designs, Paired-Acquisition Neural Factorization (PA-NF), SCORPION
  and multi-scanner canine SCC, scanner and center subspaces, strong linear
  baselines, broken-pair controls, and synthetic identifiability studies.
\item \textbf{Whole-slide aggregation.} TransnnMIL, a multibranch neural
  aggregation architecture family with canonical global correlation modeling,
  an nnMIL-style aggregation path, branch-token fusion, hierarchical spatial
  pooling, a topology branch, adaptive pruning, and coordinate-aware graph
  caching, evaluated on PANDA feature bags.
\item \textbf{Institutional aggregation.} PathologyFL, a pathology-specific
  federated-learning research framework, and FAIR-WEIGHTS-H, an auditable
  hybrid institutional-weighting protocol, exercised through dominant-site
  stress tests, corruption and label-noise stress, ordinal-shift detection,
  client heterogeneity and dropout, communication accounting, privacy probes,
  and CAMELYON17 center studies.
\item \textbf{Benchmark and provenance infrastructure.} PCam patch-level
  evaluation, PANDA and CAMELYON17 whole-slide and center evaluation, and a
  fail-closed scientific-audit system that binds every active empirical claim
  to an immutable result artifact and every architectural claim to source and
  tests.
\end{itemize}

A central methodological position of the program is that \emph{architecture},
\emph{corrected empirical evidence}, \emph{negative results}, \emph{proposed
protocols}, and \emph{pending controlled validation} must be kept rigorously
distinct. An architecture can be an authored contribution, implemented and
tested, even when its superiority over matched baselines has not been
established or has been withdrawn and awaits repaired evaluation. Simple linear
methods can outperform neural methods on bounded endpoints, and a negative
result about neural feature-space superiority does not erase the architectural
contribution of an explicit, inspectable, and swappable acquisition branch.

This manuscript is the first program-level presentation of the complete
research program. It is deliberately not a Paired-Acquisition Neural
Factorization paper: PA-NF is one of several foundational contributions, and
earlier manuscript drafts that treated it as the whole program are rejected
here in favor of an accurate, three-level, evidence-maturity-aware structure.
