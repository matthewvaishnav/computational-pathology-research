\section{Introduction}

Multi-scanner pathology data expose a basic tension: features that retain enough information to distinguish tissue regions may also encode how those regions were acquired. A separate tension emerges across institutions: the largest contributor to a training set is not necessarily the most reliable contributor under distribution shift. Both problems concern the allocation of useful signal and unwanted influence, but they require different experimental controls.

Paired-Acquisition Neural Factorization (PA-NF) addresses the first problem using matched observations of the same tissue under different scanners. Paired acquisitions reveal changes in the measurement process while keeping region identity fixed. A two-branch neural factorizer uses crossed reconstruction, within-region consistency, and acquisition-routing objectives to construct a tissue-oriented representation and an explicit acquisition-oriented representation. The measurable target is a joint one: \emph{reduced scanner accessibility with preserved tissue-region discrimination}. The claim does not depend on treating a low scanner-probe score as proof of biological purity.

The primary evidence is a registered seven-variant, five-fold, five-seed SCORPION campaign. Its equal-capacity neural comparator directly tests whether the specified paired objective changes tissue-branch scanner recoverability while maintaining retrieval. Frozen transfer across visual encoders, a five-scanner canine SCC evaluation against strong linear corrections, and exploratory retrieval studies on two distinct ScanComp slides probe the scope of that result.

The broader research framework implements whole-slide and institutional decision stages. TransnnMIL provides neural aggregation of patch features, alongside conventional multiple-instance learning comparators. PathologyFL and dominance-aware weighting test how the influence of contributing sites should respond to validation evidence. These stages share an experimental framework, but their reported SCORPION, PANDA, and CAMELYON17 outcomes were obtained from \emph{distinct evaluations}, not one jointly trained model.

The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Controlled paired-acquisition factorization.} Across 175 registered SCORPION fits, PA-NF reduces linear scanner recoverability relative to an equal-capacity neural control while satisfying the specified same-region retrieval criterion. Additional backbones, independent canine samples, and two exploratory ScanComp slides assess transfer and comparator scope.
\item \textbf{Whole-slide modeling with explicit comparators.} The pipeline includes TransnnMIL, a verified 10,611-slide PANDA feature substrate, and a completed matched seven-architecture evaluation that distinguishes implemented model capacity from measured predictive advantage.
\item \textbf{Conditional institutional aggregation.} On simulated PANDA sites, validation-triggered switching improves selected robustness endpoints under dominant-site perturbation; a separate CAMELYON17 feature-level analysis demonstrates improved held-out-center performance through alternative source weighting.
\end{enumerate}

PCam patch experiments, synthetic factor studies, spatial modeling, and reproducibility infrastructure support these components. The manuscript keeps experimental units and endpoints separate: scanner-probe balanced accuracy and region retrieval at the representation stage, slide-level performance at the aggregation stage, and site/center performance at the institutional stage. This enables a precise scientific conclusion without converting a collection of stage-specific results into an unsupported end-to-end performance claim.
