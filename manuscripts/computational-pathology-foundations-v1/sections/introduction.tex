\section{Introduction}

Computational pathology is usually presented as a sequence of separate model problems: patch classification, foundation-model feature extraction, multiple-instance learning, domain robustness, or federated learning. I built this research program from the opposite direction. The objective is an end-to-end experimental pipeline in which the same pathology system can be followed from raw or preprocessed image data, through frozen representations and scanner effects, into whole-slide aggregation, and finally into multi-institutional learning.

That pipeline matters because the errors do not stay inside one level. Scanner and preparation differences can enter the representation; a whole-slide aggregator can amplify shortcuts in those representations; and federated aggregation can then amplify whichever institution contributes the most samples rather than the most reliable signal. The research problem is therefore not only which model scores highest on one benchmark. It is how information changes as it is represented and aggregated across tissue and across institutions.

The program has four principal authored systems:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Paired-Acquisition Neural Factorization (PA-NF):} representation learning from exact same-tissue, multi-scanner observations, with separate tissue-oriented and acquisition-oriented branches.
\item \textbf{TransnnMIL:} a whole-slide multiple-instance learning architecture family combining transformer-style global correlation modeling with gated aggregation and optional spatial/topological components.
\item \textbf{PathologyFL:} a pathology-specific federated-learning framework implementing coordinator/client training, weighted aggregation, robustness mechanisms, privacy integrations, monitoring, and simulated multi-site experiments.
\item \textbf{FAIR-WEIGHTS-H:} a bounded institutional-weighting mechanism for studying when sample-count weighting should yield to quality, uncertainty, uniqueness, and integrity signals.
\end{itemize}

These systems sit inside a common data and experiment layer. PCam provides a full patch-classification benchmark and federated smoke substrate; SCORPION and multi-scanner canine SCC provide paired-acquisition scanner studies; PANDA provides 10,611 verified slide-level Phikon feature bags for whole-slide and institutional experiments; and CAMELYON17/WILDS provides 455,954 labeled examples across five centers for natural center-shift studies. Synthetic latent-factor experiments are used only where ground-truth factors are required to test mechanism questions directly.

The resulting research record contains several different kinds of evidence rather than one leaderboard. PCam measures patch-level classification; SCORPION measures whether acquisition information can be separated from tissue-oriented representations; PANDA measures slide aggregation and simulated institutional stress; and CAMELYON17 measures held-out-center behavior. The metrics are therefore reported within their own experimental settings rather than collapsed into one score.

The central contribution of this manuscript is the system itself: a computational-pathology pipeline that connects representation learning, whole-slide modeling, spatial/tissue research, and multi-institutional learning under one implementation and evaluation framework. Pretrained feature encoders are used as inputs where appropriate; the surrounding architecture, factorization methods, slide aggregators, federated infrastructure, weighting mechanisms, experiments, and evaluation code are the authored research program described here.
