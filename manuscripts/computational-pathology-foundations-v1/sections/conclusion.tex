\section{Conclusion}

Matched acquisitions provide a way to constrain scanner-associated information without discarding the correspondence between scans of the same tissue. PA-NF turns that opportunity into a two-branch representation objective whose outcomes are measured jointly: scanner recoverability from the tissue and acquisition branches, and retention of same-region identity.

In the registered 175-fit SCORPION experiment, PA-NF reduced tissue-branch linear scanner balanced accuracy by 0.3108 relative to an equal-capacity neural control while satisfying the predefined retrieval noninferiority margin. With the original checkpoints frozen, two distinct ScanComp slides showed strict three-scanner region-retrieval gains of 4.53 and 6.97 percentage points. These results establish a controlled representation advantage and exploratory external technical transfer.

The same research framework separately tests whole-slide models and institutional weighting. Its PANDA and CAMELYON17 results show that the influence of contributing sources is measurable and can be adjusted under specified shifts. The next scientific test for PA-NF is a larger independently qualified human cohort with both authenticated acquisition correspondence and a biological endpoint.
