\section{Conclusion}

This research program develops accountable neural aggregation in computational
pathology across three connected levels: representations (how patches and
foundation models encode tissue, scanner, acquisition, site, region, and dataset
artifacts), whole slides (how patch instances are aggregated into slide
predictions), and institutions (how institutional updates are aggregated into
collaborative models). The program's contributions span neural architectures
(Paired-Acquisition Neural Factorization and TransnnMIL), paired-data designs
(exact same-region scanner pairs and their controls), institutional-weighting
protocols (PathologyFL and FAIR-WEIGHTS-H), stress tests and benchmarks (PCam,
PANDA, CAMELYON17), and a fail-closed provenance and scientific-audit system
that binds every active claim to an immutable artifact.

The evidence maturity is heterogeneous and is stated explicitly. Corrected
paired-scanner evidence supports partial structured separation and paired
supervision but not neural feature-space superiority; the corrected
fixed-estimand adjudication found no neural feature-space increment, and
synthetic accessibility effects did not transport. TransnnMIL is an implemented
architecture whose corrected matched-control performance evaluation remains
pending. PathologyFL and FAIR-WEIGHTS-H provide executable, auditable federated
research and institutional weighting without demonstrated clinical or fairness
superiority.

The shared contribution is a multi-level framework for building and auditing
accountable neural aggregation in computational pathology --- one in which
architecture, corrected empirical evidence, negative results, proposed
protocols, and pending controlled validation are kept rigorously distinct, and
in which the aggregation choices at each level are treated as a connected system
rather than separate engineering problems.
