\section{Conclusion}

Paired-Acquisition Neural Factorization demonstrates that matched multi-scanner observations can alter measured scanner accessibility without sacrificing tissue-region retrieval under a controlled neural comparison. The registered 175-fit SCORPION experiment lowers tissue-branch linear scanner balanced accuracy by 0.3108 relative to an equal-capacity control while satisfying the prespecified retrieval noninferiority requirement. Frozen-checkpoint evaluation on two distinct ScanComp slides further improves strict cross-scanner region retrieval by 4.53 and 6.97 percentage points, providing exploratory external technical replication.

The broader computational pathology framework also evaluates whole-slide prediction and institutional source influence under explicit controls. In simulated PANDA studies, validation-triggered aggregation improves performance under specified dominant-site shifts; CAMELYON17 source-weighting experiments independently demonstrate gains on an unseen center.

The central contribution is a set of reproducible, comparator-defined interventions on acquisition-associated information and institutional influence. Larger independently qualified cohorts and biological ground truth remain necessary to establish the clinical significance of the representation findings.
