\textbf{Abstract.}
Scanner-dependent variation and institutional sampling can shape the signals available to computational pathology models. We investigate whether paired acquisitions can constrain scanner-associated information without sacrificing tissue-region discrimination, and whether conditional source weighting can improve robustness when institutional influence becomes misaligned with task performance.

Paired-Acquisition Neural Factorization (PA-NF) maps same-tissue multi-scanner features into tissue-oriented and acquisition-oriented branches. In a registered 175-fit SCORPION study spanning 48 human H\&E slides, 480 matched tissue regions and five scanners, PA-NF reduced tissue-branch linear scanner balanced accuracy by 0.3108 relative to an equal-capacity neural control (95\% fold/slide bootstrap interval $[-0.3346,-0.2858]$), while meeting the prespecified 0.02 same-region retrieval noninferiority margin. Scanner information remained accessible in the explicit acquisition branch. Applying 25 original frozen checkpoints to two distinct ScanComp slides improved three-scanner strict same-region top-1 retrieval from 92.97\% to 97.50\% and from 81.25\% to 88.22\%, respectively. These external panels are exploratory because eligibility depended on frozen registration quality control.

The framework also contains whole-slide aggregation and institutional learning components evaluated in separate studies. A validation-triggered detector improved global quadratic weighted kappa over FedAvg by 0.008009 and 0.007482 under 25\% and 35\% dominant-site label corruption in simulated PANDA sites. In a CAMELYON17 held-out-center feature-level study, equal-client weighting increased accuracy from 0.8312 to 0.9132 relative to sample-proportional weighting on frozen ImageNet features.

Together, the results establish controlled representation-level scanner-accessibility and conditional source-weighting effects across distinct pathology experiments. They motivate explicit treatment of acquisition and institutional variation without requiring a claim of complete biological factor identifiability or clinical validation.
