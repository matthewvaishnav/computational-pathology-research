\textbf{Abstract.}
Scanner acquisition and institutional sampling alter which signals a computational pathology model can exploit. We investigate these effects at three levels: paired-scanner representation learning, whole-slide aggregation, and institutional source weighting. The levels share a research framework but are evaluated in distinct experiments, not as a jointly trained end-to-end predictor.

The primary representation experiment uses 48 human H\&E SCORPION slides (480 matched regions, five scanners) in a registered 175-fit campaign. Against an equal-capacity two-branch neural control, Paired-Acquisition Neural Factorization (PA-NF) reduced tissue-branch linear scanner balanced accuracy by 0.3108 (95\% fold/slide bootstrap interval $[-0.3346,-0.2858]$), while satisfying the prespecified 0.02 noninferiority margin for same-region retrieval. Scanner information remained accessible in the acquisition branch. On two distinct, registration-qualified ScanComp slides, the original frozen checkpoints increased three-scanner strict same-region top-1 retrieval from 92.97\% to 97.50\% and from 81.25\% to 88.22\%. These post-QC-selected panels provide exploratory technical transfer, not four-scanner confirmation or evidence of biological purity. Independent canine experiments further show that simple scanner-removal baselines remain competitive on a harder category/retrieval frontier.

The whole-slide stage was tested separately using PANDA Phikon feature bags. In a completed 35-fit, seven-architecture matched comparison, repaired TransnnMIL did not outperform TransMIL (mean confirmation QWK difference $-0.013631$; zero of five favorable paired seeds). At the institutional stage, a dominance-aware rule improved global QWK over FedAvg by 0.008009 and 0.007482 under 25\% and 35\% dominant-site label corruption, respectively. In a separate CAMELYON17 feature-level proxy, equal-client source weighting improved held-out-center accuracy from 0.8312 to 0.9132 on frozen ImageNet features.

These results support comparator-specific changes in measured scanner accessibility and source influence. They do not establish complete biological--acquisition identifiability, a causal benefit propagated across all three stages, or clinical validity.
