\section*{Abstract}

Computational-pathology neural systems aggregate information across several
levels: patches are transformed into representations, patch instances are
aggregated into whole-slide predictions, and institutional updates are
aggregated into collaborative models. At each level, nuisance variation,
shortcut signals, optimization failures, and institutional imbalance can be
preserved or amplified. This research program develops neural architectures,
paired-data designs, institutional-weighting protocols, stress tests, and
provenance controls for auditing and improving those aggregation stages.

At the representation level, we introduce Paired-Acquisition Neural
Factorization (PA-NF), a paired-data architecture with an explicit
tissue-oriented biological branch and an explicit acquisition branch trained on
exact same-region multi-scanner pairs, together with strong linear baselines
(centroid/QR, PCA, paired-linear), broken-pair controls, and synthetic
identifiability studies. At the whole-slide level, we introduce TransnnMIL, a
multibranch neural aggregation architecture family with canonical global
correlation modeling, an nnMIL-style aggregation path, branch-token fusion,
hierarchical spatial pooling, a topology branch, adaptive pruning, and
coordinate-aware graph caching. At the institutional level, we develop
PathologyFL, a pathology-specific federated-learning research framework, and
FAIR-WEIGHTS-H, a proposed and partially implemented auditable hybrid
institutional-weighting protocol that specifies, but has not yet fully
implemented, a separation of training, validation, and monitoring signals.

These components are connected by benchmark infrastructure (PCam patch
evaluation), whole-slide benchmarks (PANDA), multi-center benchmarks
(CAMELYON17), and a fail-closed scientific-audit and provenance system that
binds every active empirical claim to an immutable result artifact.

The evidence maturity differs sharply across components and is stated
explicitly. Corrected paired-scanner experiments support partial structured
separation and paired supervision, but not neural feature-space superiority:
no neural feature-space increment over simple scanner-removal baselines was
supported under the corrected fixed five-category estimand, B64 increased
retrieval and scanner recoverability without category gain, and synthetic
accessibility effects did not transport to corrected real category
accessibility. TransnnMIL is an implemented architecture whose repaired
matched-control performance evaluation remains pending. PathologyFL and
FAIR-WEIGHTS-H support executable pathology-specific federated research and
auditable institutional weighting without demonstrated clinical or fairness
superiority.

The shared contribution is a multi-level framework for building and auditing
accountable neural aggregation in computational pathology, in which
architecture, corrected empirical evidence, negative results, proposed
protocols, and pending controlled validation are kept rigorously distinct.
