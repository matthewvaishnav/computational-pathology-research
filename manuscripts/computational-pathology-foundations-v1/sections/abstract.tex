\textbf{Abstract.}
A scanner-insensitive pathology representation is useful only if it preserves the tissue distinctions needed for analysis. We introduce Paired-Acquisition Neural Factorization (PA-NF), which learns tissue-oriented and acquisition-oriented feature branches from repeated scans of the same tissue region. Its paired-region contrastive objective, joint feature reconstruction, and scanner-related constraints make acquisition recoverability and region preservation jointly measurable.

In a registered SCORPION study of 48 human H\&E slides, 480 matched regions and five scanners, 175 capacity-matched fits showed a 0.3108 reduction in tissue-branch scanner balanced accuracy against an equal-capacity neural control (95\% fold/slide interval $[-0.3346,-0.2858]$). Same-region retrieval met the prespecified 0.02 noninferiority margin, while acquisition-branch scanner recoverability increased. With all 25 PA-NF checkpoints frozen, strict cross-scanner retrieval improved from 92.97\% to 97.50\% on ScanComp slide \texttt{kwo930}, and from 81.25\% to 88.22\% on \texttt{jye249}; both are exploratory three-scanner panels admitted through fixed registration criteria.

The broader framework separately examines slide prediction and institutional influence. In simulated PANDA sites, validation-triggered weighting improved global quadratic weighted kappa over FedAvg by 0.008009 and 0.007482 under 25\% and 35\% dominant-site label corruption. In a CAMELYON17 feature-level center-shift study, equal-client weighting improved held-out-center accuracy from 0.8312 to 0.9132. Together, the experiments establish measurable changes in scanner accessibility, region retrieval, and source influence under distinct controlled comparisons, without implying clinical validation of their untested joint composition.
