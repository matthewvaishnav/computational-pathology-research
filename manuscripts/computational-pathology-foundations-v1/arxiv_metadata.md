## Abstract

Computational-pathology systems aggregate information at several distinct levels: representation formation from image patches, whole-slide aggregation from instance bags, and institutional aggregation across data sources or sites. We present a connected research program spanning these levels while separating implemented architecture from validated empirical performance. At the representation level, Paired-Acquisition Neural Factorization uses matched multi-scanner acquisitions to allocate scanner-associated information between tissue-oriented and acquisition branches. Corrected evidence supports partial structured separation under tested protocols and includes negative results against strong simple scanner-removal baselines. At the whole-slide level, TransnnMIL defines authored multibranch aggregation families whose repaired implementation is documented while controlled superiority testing remains pending. At the institutional level, PathologyFL provides research infrastructure and FAIR-WEIGHTS-H specifies a contribution- and dominance-aware weighting protocol without claiming prospective fairness or performance superiority. PCam, PANDA, CAMELYON17, synthetic mechanism studies, immutable evidence packages, and a living claim boundary provide benchmark and provenance context. The contribution is a technically explicit foundations account of how these systems are constructed, what has been validated, what remains pending, and which interpretations are prohibited.
