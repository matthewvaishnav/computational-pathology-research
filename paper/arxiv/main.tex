Independent Researcher}

\date{June 17, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
This paper reports an independent computational-pathology research program rather than a single model or experiment. The program studies four connected failure surfaces: what information is encoded in patch and region representations, how patch evidence is aggregated into whole-slide predictions, how institutional influence is assigned during federated learning, and how communication, privacy perturbation, and infrastructure constraints affect the resulting system. The public record includes PathoAlign neural identifiability studies, real paired-scanner validation on SCORPION, PANDA whole-slide multiple-instance learning, dominant-site federated stress tests, CAMELYON17 external-center aggregation studies, PCam patch validation, and bounded systems analyses.

The newest and strongest locked result uses SCORPION: 480 aligned tissue regions from 48 original human H\&E slides, each scanned on five devices, yielding 2,400 real-tissue images \citep{ryu2025scorpion}. Frozen-feature audits found strong scanner predictability despite high tissue retention in ImageNet ResNet50, DINOv2-Base, and pathology-native Phikon. A PathoAlign objective calibrated only on DINOv2 development data reduced biological scanner-probe accuracy from 0.7825 to 0.3989 in five-fold original-slide evaluation while improving paired agreement and preserving near-perfect tissue retrieval. Without backbone-specific tuning, the frozen objective transferred to Phikon and ResNet50. Across the two unseen backbones, the slide-blocked scanner-probe difference was $-0.4019$ with 95\% bootstrap interval $[-0.4145,-0.3895]$, favorable on all 48 slides, while scanner identity remained strongly decodable from a compact acquisition branch.

The SCORPION study builds on earlier PathoAlign negative results showing that lower site leakage does not automatically improve tumor prediction and that unconditional removal of a learned site component can erase tumor-predictive biology. Controlled latent-factor studies then treated observational samples, unique paired anchors, and repeated paired exposure as distinct resources, producing failure/recovery phase maps, censored threshold analysis, and matched-budget allocation results. The broader record also includes 10,611 verified PANDA Phikon feature bags for mean pooling, AttentionMIL, and TransnnMIL; 15-seed dominant-site stress and detector-transfer studies; 455,954 CAMELYON17/WILDS examples across five centers; PCam test and bootstrap validation; and bounded communication, privacy-noise, and infrastructure-friction analyses.

The studies use different datasets, units of analysis, and claim boundaries. None establishes clinical validation, diagnostic deployment readiness, universal disentanglement, formal differential privacy, or improved patient outcomes. The supported conclusion is narrower: representation formation, slide aggregation, institutional influence, and systems constraints are separate sources of failure and should be tested independently before an integrated computational-pathology system is trusted.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Computational pathology systems learn across a hierarchy. Image patches are encoded into feature representations; patch evidence is aggregated into slide- or patient-level predictions; multi-institutional training combines local models or updates; and the complete process must operate under communication, privacy, hardware, and governance constraints. A strong aggregate score can conceal failure at any one of these levels.

At the representation level, scanner, staining, preparation, institution, and tissue morphology can be statistically entangled. Removing site information indiscriminately can erase useful biology. At the whole-slide level, thousands of correlated patch embeddings must be combined under weak supervision. At the federation level, sample-weighted FedAvg silently assumes that more local samples should grant more authority over the global model \citep{mcmahan2017communication}. At the systems level, nominally successful algorithms may remain impractical because of communication cost, privacy leakage, dropout, or straggler delay.

The repository therefore contains several distinct but connected study families:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{PathoAlign} studies biological--acquisition identifiability, negative results from unconditional site removal, paired interventions, synthetic recovery regimes, and real paired-scanner factorization.
  \item \textbf{Whole-slide modeling} studies mean pooling, gated AttentionMIL, and TransnnMIL on verified PANDA pathology foundation-model feature bags.
  \item \textbf{PathologyFL and site-signal auditing} study sample-volume dominance, fixed detector transfer, diagnostic ablation, and external-center aggregation.
  \item \textbf{Patch and systems validation} include PCam classification, uncertainty and threshold analysis, communication accounting, privacy-noise stress, and infrastructure-friction simulation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Working thesis}
Pathology systems should not be trusted merely because they predict well in one split. They require explicit audits of what information is retained, what evidence dominates a slide prediction, which institutions control a shared model, and whether the implementation remains reliable under external-center and systems stress.

\section{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Real paired-acquisition PathoAlign.} A multi-backbone scanner-entanglement audit and frozen factorization study on real human tissue across DINOv2, Phikon, and ResNet50.
  \item \textbf{Negative-result and identifiability program.} Locked adversarial confirmation, factorized-model selection, unconditional-subtraction falsification, controlled phase maps, censored recovery thresholds, and matched-budget anchor-diversity experiments.
  \item \textbf{PANDA whole-slide studies.} Mean-pooled, AttentionMIL, and repeated-seed TransnnMIL experiments over 10,611 verified Phikon feature bags, including an 18-run optimization-stability grid.
  \item \textbf{Federated site-signal studies.} Dominant-site label-noise stress, frozen detector transfer to conservative ordinal threshold shift, leave-one-diagnostic-family-out ablation, and calibration-neighborhood sensitivity.
  \item \textbf{Natural external-center validation.} Two CAMELYON17 feature families comparing sample-proportional, equal-client, dominant-source-downweighted, and detector-controlled policies on an unseen center.
  \item \textbf{Patch and systems evidence.} PCam test/bootstrap/threshold validation plus bounded communication, privacy-noise, and infrastructure-friction studies.
  \item \textbf{Study-specific claim boundaries.} Every experiment is reported with its own dataset, unit of analysis, limitations, and unsupported claims rather than being compressed into one platform-performance statement.
\end{enumerate}

\section{SCORPION paired-scanner PathoAlign study}
\subsection{Dataset and frozen representation audit}
The public SCORPION release contains 48 original H\&E slides, ten aligned regions per slide, and one image from each of five scanners for every region: AT2, GT450, DP200, P1000, and B300 \citep{ryu2025scorpion}. This yields 480 tissue regions and 2,400 images. The original slide is the split, bootstrap, and permutation unit.

Three representation families were audited: ImageNet-pretrained ResNet50 \citep{he2016deep}, self-supervised DINOv2-Base \citep{oquab2023dinov2}, and pathology-native Phikon \citep{filiot2023scaling}. A logistic scanner probe was trained on slide-disjoint training data and evaluated on held-out slides. Tissue identity was measured with bidirectional cross-scanner region retrieval.

Scanner identity is therefore not confined to one architecture or pretraining domain. Phikon retained the strongest scanner signal, even though it was pretrained specifically for pathology. DINOv2 was selected as the development substrate because tissue retrieval was nearly saturated while residual scanner information remained substantial.

\subsection{PathoAlign objective}
Let $x_{r,s}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$ denote the frozen feature for tissue region $r$ scanned by scanner $s$. PathoAlign learns a biological representation $z_b=f_b(x_{r,s})\in\mathbb{R}^{256}$ and an acquisition representation $z_a=f_a(x_{r,s})\in\mathbb{R}^{64}$. The biological branch is trained with multi-positive same-region contrastive learning in batches containing all five scanner views of each selected region.

The frozen objective adds a direct normalized scanner-dependence penalty between biological features and one-hot scanner labels,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dep}}
  =\left\|
  \frac{1}{B-1}\widetilde Z_b^{\top}\widetilde Y_s
  \right\|_F^2,
\end{equation}
plus scanner prediction from the acquisition branch, gradient reversal against scanner prediction from biology, reconstruction, variance control, and biological/acquisition cross-covariance. The frozen weights are scanner adversary 0.5, scanner acquisition 0.5, direct scanner-dependence 20.0, cross-covariance 0.05, reconstruction 1.0, variance 1.0, and biological covariance 0.01. The comparison method uses the same biological projection, paired contrastive loss, reconstruction, variance, and covariance terms, but no acquisition branch or scanner-separation losses.

\subsection{Frozen evaluation protocol}
DINOv2 development was separated into bounded calibration, independent-seed confirmation, and final five-fold original-slide evaluation. The selected dependence weight of 20 was frozen before final testing. Cross-backbone transfer was preregistered before inspecting Phikon or ResNet50 outcomes. Both transfer backbones used new shared seeds 701--705, the same five rotating folds, and exactly the same objective and training schedule.

Each model used 75 epochs, region batch size 32, AdamW learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, and weight decay $10^{-4}$. Optimization seeds were averaged within slide. Bootstrap intervals and sign-flip tests used the 48 original slides as matched blocks. Patches, regions, scanner views, and backbone repetitions were not treated as independent biological samples.

\section{Limitations}
The SCORPION evidence is limited to 48 original slides, five scanners, sampled aligned regions, and three frozen feature families. Cross-backbone transfer rules out a narrow DINOv2-specific explanation, but it does not establish transfer to a new dataset, laboratory, tissue type, stain, preparation workflow, magnification, or scanner model outside SCORPION. Tissue-region retrieval is an identity-retention diagnostic, not a clinical endpoint, and a linear probe cannot certify removal of all scanner information.

The synthetic PathoAlign studies use known latent factors precisely because identifiability cannot be directly measured on ordinary real data. Their phase maps and recovery thresholds are local to the tested generators, metrics, methods, and budgets. The matched-budget study did not cross the historical recovery threshold.

The PANDA studies use precomputed Phikon feature bags and retrospective validation. TransnnMIL still requires architecture-level ablations, external cohorts, calibration analysis, and comparisons under strictly matched compute. The strongest federated PANDA experiments use simulated clients and controlled label-process shifts rather than actual hospital federation.

CAMELYON17 supplies natural center shift but remains feature-level rather than end-to-end federated WSI training. Communication is analytically bounded rather than measured over a real wide-area network. The privacy-noise study is not differential privacy, and the infrastructure study is a simulation rather than deployment evidence. PCam is a patch-level benchmark and cannot establish whole-slide or clinical performance.

No study evaluates treatment decisions, prospective pathologist workflow, patient outcomes, regulatory readiness, or a complete end-to-end PathoAlign--TransnnMIL--PathologyFL clinical system.

\section{Discussion}
The studies expose related forms of hidden authority. In a representation, scanner- or site-associated directions can dominate what a downstream model learns. In a whole-slide learner, pooling or attention determines which tissue regions dominate the prediction. In federation, sample count determines which institution dominates the update unless the rule is explicitly audited. In systems operation, communication, noise, dropout, and straggler behavior determine which theoretical methods are practical.

The research progression is important. Early adversarial PathoAlign experiments showed that lower site leakage alone was not a diagnostic win. Factorized modeling and unconditional subtraction then showed that site-associated components can carry tumor information. Controlled paired-intervention experiments identified conditions under which biological and acquisition factors become recoverable. SCORPION moved that idea to real paired scanner observations and demonstrated frozen cross-backbone transfer. PANDA and CAMELYON17 then test later stages of the learning pipeline: slide aggregation and institutional influence.

The broader value is therefore not a claim that one architecture solves computational pathology. It is a study program that separates questions which are often conflated: what a representation contains, whether that information is identifiable, how slide evidence is combined, how institutions influence a shared model, and whether the system remains viable under operational constraints.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper reports a computational-pathology research program with distinct, reproducible studies at the patch, representation, slide, institution, external-center, and systems levels. The strongest current result is frozen paired-scanner PathoAlign factorization across three substantially different pretrained representation families on real human histopathology. That result is supported by earlier falsification studies and controlled resource-allocation experiments rather than standing alone.

The wider record includes PCam patch validation, PANDA whole-slide MIL, TransnnMIL stabilization, dominant-site federated stress, fixed detector transfer, diagnostic ablation, CAMELYON17 external-center aggregation, communication accounting, privacy-noise stress, and infrastructure simulation. Together, these studies support one bounded thesis: reliable computational pathology requires separate audits of representation content, biological retention, slide aggregation, institutional influence, external generalization, and implementation constraints. The complete clinically validated system remains future work.
