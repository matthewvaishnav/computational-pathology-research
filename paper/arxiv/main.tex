\begin{abstract}
This paper summarizes a computational-pathology research program with three linked empirical lines: PathoAlign for paired-acquisition representation factorization on SCORPION, TransnnMIL for weakly supervised whole-slide modeling on PANDA, and PathologyFL for external-center/federated validation on CAMELYON17. The strongest locked result is PathoAlign, but PANDA and CAMELYON17 are substantive parts of the public research record and are reported explicitly rather than treated as footnotes.

PathoAlign addresses the fact that pathology representations can preserve tissue identity while simultaneously encoding scanner, staining, preparation, and institutional acquisition effects. The main paired-scanner study uses SCORPION: 480 aligned regions from 48 original human H\&E slides, each scanned on five devices, yielding 2,400 real-tissue image patches \citep{ryu2025scorpion}. Frozen-feature audits found strong scanner predictability despite high tissue retention in ImageNet ResNet50, DINOv2-Base, and pathology-native Phikon. PathoAlign combines multi-positive same-region contrastive learning, a direct scanner-dependence penalty in biology, scanner prediction in the acquisition branch, gradient reversal, reconstruction, variance control, and cross-factor decorrelation.

The objective was calibrated only on DINOv2 development data, then frozen. Five-fold original-slide evaluation reduced biological scanner-probe accuracy from 0.7825 to 0.3989 relative to paired consistency while improving mean paired cosine by 0.0310 and preserving near-perfect tissue retrieval. Without any backbone-specific tuning, the same objective transferred to Phikon and ResNet50. Scanner-probe accuracy decreased by 0.4353 on Phikon and 0.3685 on ResNet50; mean paired cosine increased by 0.0900 and 0.0255, respectively; retrieval was preserved or improved. Across the two transfer backbones, the repeated-measures slide-blocked scanner-probe difference was $-0.4019$ with 95\% bootstrap interval $[-0.4145,-0.3895]$, favorable on all 48 slides. Scanner identity remained strongly decodable from the compact acquisition branch, supporting factor separation rather than representational erasure.

The public PDF also reports PANDA/TransnnMIL whole-slide grading and CAMELYON17/PathologyFL external-center aggregation results with their own claim boundaries. None of these results establish clinical validation, diagnostic deployment readiness, or universal biological/acquisition identifiability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Digital pathology images contain both tissue morphology and acquisition-specific variation. Scanner optics, color response, compression, staining, preparation, and local workflow can all influence the representation learned from a patch. These factors are not necessarily orthogonal to biological content. A model may therefore retrieve the correct tissue or perform well on a downstream task while still encoding scanner identity, and indiscriminate nuisance removal can erase biologically useful information.

The repository has three major empirical threads. PathoAlign studies representation factorization under paired scanner interventions. TransnnMIL studies whole-slide aggregation and ordinal grading from pathology foundation-model feature bags. PathologyFL studies external-center generalization and auditable institutional influence under distribution shift. They are related parts of one computational-pathology program, but they have different datasets, units of analysis, metrics, and claim boundaries.

\subsection*{Working thesis}
Biology-preserving paired interventions can supervise a representation in which tissue identity is retained while acquisition identity becomes substantially less recoverable. Whole-slide aggregation and external-center validation then test later stages of the pathology learning pipeline. These stages should be reported together but not conflated.

\section{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{PathoAlign / SCORPION:} a real-tissue multi-backbone scanner-entanglement audit and frozen paired-acquisition factorization across DINOv2, Phikon, and ResNet50.
  \item \textbf{TransnnMIL / PANDA:} weakly supervised whole-slide modeling on 10,611 verified PANDA Phikon feature bags, including mean-pooled, AttentionMIL, and TransnnMIL baselines.
  \item \textbf{PathologyFL / CAMELYON17:} external-center feature-level validation over 455,954 examples from five centers, comparing sample-proportional, equal-client, and dominant-source-downweighted aggregation.
  \item \textbf{Claim-boundary discipline:} the paper separates representation diagnostics, slide-level grading, and external-center aggregation rather than using one result to overstate another.
\end{enumerate}

\section{SCORPION paired-scanner PathoAlign study}
\subsection{Dataset and frozen representation audit}
The public SCORPION release contains 48 original H\&E slides, ten aligned regions per slide, and one image from each of five scanners for every region: AT2, GT450, DP200, P1000, and B300 \citep{ryu2025scorpion}. This yields 480 tissue regions and 2,400 images. The original slide is the split, bootstrap, and permutation unit.

Three representation families were audited: ImageNet-pretrained ResNet50 \citep{he2016deep}, self-supervised DINOv2-Base \citep{oquab2023dinov2}, and pathology-native Phikon \citep{filiot2023scaling}. A logistic scanner probe was trained on slide-disjoint training data and evaluated on held-out slides. Tissue identity was measured with bidirectional cross-scanner region retrieval.

Scanner identity is therefore not confined to one architecture or pretraining domain. Phikon retained the strongest scanner signal, even though it was pretrained specifically for pathology. DINOv2 was selected as the development substrate because tissue retrieval was nearly saturated while residual scanner information remained substantial.

\subsection{PathoAlign objective}
Let $x_{r,s}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$ denote the frozen feature for tissue region $r$ scanned by scanner $s$. PathoAlign learns a biological representation $z_b=f_b(x_{r,s})\in\mathbb{R}^{256}$ and an acquisition representation $z_a=f_a(x_{r,s})\in\mathbb{R}^{64}$. The biological branch is trained with multi-positive same-region contrastive learning in batches containing all five scanner views of each selected region.

The frozen objective adds a direct normalized scanner-dependence penalty between biological features and one-hot scanner labels,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dep}}
  =\left\|
  \frac{1}{B-1}\widetilde Z_b^{\top}\widetilde Y_s
  \right\|_F^2,
\end{equation}
plus scanner prediction from the acquisition branch, gradient reversal against scanner prediction from biology, reconstruction, variance control, and biological/acquisition cross-covariance. The frozen weights are scanner adversary 0.5, scanner acquisition 0.5, direct scanner-dependence 20.0, cross-covariance 0.05, reconstruction 1.0, variance 1.0, and biological covariance 0.01. The comparison method uses the same biological projection, paired contrastive loss, reconstruction, variance, and covariance terms, but no acquisition branch or scanner-separation losses.

\subsection{Frozen evaluation protocol}
DINOv2 development was separated into bounded calibration, independent-seed confirmation, and final five-fold original-slide evaluation. The selected dependence weight of 20 was frozen before final testing. Cross-backbone transfer was preregistered before inspecting Phikon or ResNet50 outcomes. Both transfer backbones used new shared seeds 701--705, the same five rotating folds, and exactly the same objective and training schedule.

Each model used 75 epochs, region batch size 32, AdamW learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, and weight decay $10^{-4}$. Optimization seeds were averaged within slide. Bootstrap intervals and sign-flip tests used the 48 original slides as matched blocks. Patches, regions, scanner views, and backbone repetitions were not treated as independent biological samples.

\section{Supporting identifiability studies}
\subsection{Negative results motivating paired supervision}
Earlier PathoAlign experiments used adversarial center removal and factorized diagnostic/site branches on pathology-derived representations. A locked adversarial confirmation reduced site leakage only slightly and did not improve held-out tumor accuracy. Unconditional subtraction was falsified because the learned acquisition-associated component remained strongly tumor-predictive. These failures motivate the present design: acquisition information should not be removed solely because it predicts site; biology-preserving pairing is needed to determine what should remain stable.

\subsection{Synthetic recovery regimes}
Controlled generators with known biological and acquisition factors varied observational sample size, paired-anchor count, repeated exposure, biological--acquisition overlap, linear versus nonlinear mixing, method, and seed. The phase maps show that paired interventions and ordinary observations are distinct resources. Recovery becomes harder as overlap and nonlinearity increase. Matched-budget experiments show that total paired exposure is the dominant resource over the tested range, with a smaller benefit from anchor diversity and diminishing returns beyond approximately 100 unique anchors.

\section{Limitations}
The central PathoAlign evidence is limited to the SCORPION benchmark, its 48 original slides, five scanners, sampled regions, and three frozen feature families. Cross-backbone transfer rules out a narrow DINOv2-specific explanation, but it does not establish transfer to a new dataset, laboratory, tissue type, stain, preparation workflow, magnification, or scanner model outside SCORPION.

Tissue-region retrieval is an identity-retention diagnostic, not a clinical endpoint. The PANDA experiments are feature-bag validation results rather than clinical prostate grading deployment. The CAMELYON17 experiments are feature-level external-center aggregation baselines rather than a prospective federated hospital system. None of the reported work evaluates treatment decisions, patient outcomes, regulatory readiness, or clinical scanner equivalence.

Linear probes measure recoverable scanner information under a specified model class; they do not prove that all scanner information has been removed. Scanner prediction in biology remains above chance, and acquisition factors retain some tissue identity. The result is strong separation, not perfect disentanglement or a universal identifiability theorem.

\section{Discussion}
PathoAlign shows that pathology-specific pretraining does not automatically remove acquisition information. Phikon had the strongest baseline scanner probe, while DINOv2 had nearly perfect tissue retrieval and substantial scanner leakage. ResNet50 began from a weaker tissue representation but still encoded scanner identity. The unchanged transfer result across all three feature families supports a representation-family rather than backbone-specific finding.

The broader program connects this representation result to whole-slide and external-center problems. PANDA/TransnnMIL tests whether slide-level aggregation can improve ordinal grading from pathology feature bags. CAMELYON17/PathologyFL tests whether sample-proportional institutional influence and source-center fit predict held-out-center generalization. These lines should remain visible because they define the larger research direction, but their claims are separate from the paired-scanner factorization result.

\section{Conclusion}
The supported conclusion is narrow but substantive: biology-preserving paired acquisitions can supervise a factorization that transfers across substantially different pretrained representation families on real human histopathology. In the broader research program, this sits alongside PANDA whole-slide modeling and CAMELYON17 external-center validation. Together, the work studies representation separation, slide aggregation, and institutional generalization without claiming clinical validation or universal robustness.
