\title{PathoAlign: Paired-Acquisition Neural Identifiability for Computational Pathology\\
Separating Scanner Signal from Tissue Identity on Real Human Histopathology}

\author{Matthew Vaishnav\\
Independent Researcher}

\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Computational-pathology representations can encode tissue morphology together with scanner, staining, preparation, and institutional acquisition effects. High task or retrieval performance does not establish that these factors are separated. I present PathoAlign, a neural identifiability program centered on biology-preserving paired acquisitions: the same tissue is observed under different acquisition systems, providing direct supervision for information that should remain biologically stable and information that may be isolated as acquisition-specific.

The main empirical study uses the public SCORPION benchmark, which contains 480 spatially aligned tissue regions from 48 original H\&E slides, each scanned on five scanners for 2,400 real-human-tissue image patches \citep{ryu2025scorpion}. Frozen-feature audits show that scanner identity remains strongly linearly decodable across three representation families despite high cross-scanner tissue retention: 75.6\% for ImageNet ResNet50, 84.0\% for DINOv2-Base, and 96.4\% for the pathology-native Phikon encoder. PathoAlign factorizes a frozen representation into biological and acquisition branches using multi-positive same-region contrastive learning, direct scanner-dependence suppression, scanner prediction in the acquisition branch, cross-factor decorrelation, reconstruction, and collapse controls.

After calibration on one development partition, the objective was frozen and evaluated over five rotating original-slide test folds with five new optimization seeds per fold. Relative to paired consistency alone, PathoAlign reduced biological scanner-probe accuracy from 0.7825 to 0.3989, a slide-blocked difference of $-0.3854$ with 95\% bootstrap interval $[-0.4079,-0.3622]$. Mean and worst scanner-pair cosine agreement improved by approximately 0.031, while mean and worst cross-scanner tissue retrieval changed by less than 0.0005 and remained above 0.9987. The scanner-probe and cosine effects were favorable on all 48 original slides. The acquisition branch retained scanner identity at 0.8602 accuracy while carrying only 0.1042 mean tissue retrieval, supporting factor separation rather than representational collapse. Synthetic recovery studies and a secondary federated stress test provide supporting evidence about intervention budgets and influence failures, but the central result is a frozen out-of-fold paired-scanner separation result on real human histopathology.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Digital pathology models operate on images whose appearance depends not only on tissue morphology but also on scanners, staining, compression, preparation, and local workflow. These acquisition effects may coexist with biological information inside the same representation. A model can therefore achieve strong prediction or tissue retrieval while still encoding acquisition identity, and an invariance objective can remove useful biology if nuisance and morphology overlap.

Most observational datasets do not identify which acquisition-associated directions are nuisance and which carry tissue information. Biology-preserving paired acquisitions change that information structure. When the same tissue region is scanned on multiple devices, tissue identity is held fixed while scanner conditions change. This provides a controlled intervention for testing and learning biological stability rather than guessing invariance from unpaired center labels.

SCORPION was designed for this purpose: 480 aligned tissue regions from 48 original H\&E slides were scanned on five devices, yielding 2,400 paired patches \citep{ryu2025scorpion}. The dataset enables a direct question: can a learned biological representation preserve the identity of the same tissue across scanners while making scanner identity difficult to recover, and can a separate acquisition representation retain the removed scanner information?

PathoAlign treats this as a factorization and identifiability problem rather than a generic domain-generalization score. The program combines real paired-scanner experiments, controlled synthetic studies with known latent factors, and negative results showing that unconditional site removal can erase biological information. A smaller federated component examines a related but secondary question: whether sample count should automatically determine model influence when site-specific training signals differ.

\subsection*{Working thesis}
Paired interventions provide the strongest available supervision for separating biological content from acquisition variation. A successful method should reduce acquisition predictability in the biological representation, preserve tissue identity and representation rank, and retain acquisition information in a distinct factor rather than destroying it.

\section{Contributions}
The principal contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{A real-tissue multi-backbone scanner-entanglement audit.} ResNet50, DINOv2-Base \citep{oquab2023dinov2}, and Phikon \citep{filiot2023scaling} all preserve tissue identity while retaining strong scanner information on SCORPION.
  \item \textbf{A paired-acquisition PathoAlign objective.} The method combines same-region multi-positive contrastive learning with a biological branch, a scanner-predictive acquisition branch, direct scanner-dependence suppression in biology, gradient reversal, reconstruction, variance control, and cross-factor decorrelation.
  \item \textbf{A locked five-fold result on unseen original slides.} Hyperparameters selected on one development partition were frozen before five rotating slide-level test folds, using new seeds and no validation checkpoint selection.
  \item \textbf{A slide-blocked separation result.} PathoAlign substantially reduces biological scanner leakage and improves same-tissue paired agreement while preserving near-perfect cross-scanner tissue retrieval on all 48 original slides.
  \item \textbf{A factor-retention audit.} Scanner identity remains strongly decodable in the compact acquisition branch, while tissue identity is concentrated in the biological branch and all biological dimensions remain active.
  \item \textbf{Supporting synthetic identifiability maps.} Controlled experiments vary observational data, paired-anchor diversity, repeated exposure, overlap, nonlinearity, method, and optimization budget to characterize recovery boundaries and resource allocation.
  \item \textbf{Negative-result and claim-boundary discipline.} Earlier adversarial and subtraction experiments show that lower site leakage alone does not guarantee better diagnosis, and the present real-tissue result is not presented as clinical validation.
  \item \textbf{A secondary federated influence stress test.} Dominant-client experiments illustrate that sample volume is an influence rule, not a complete measure of task alignment; this component is complementary rather than the paper's primary focus.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Real paired-scanner benchmark}
\subsection{SCORPION structure and leakage boundary}
The SCORPION release contains 48 original H\&E slides, ten aligned regions per slide, and one image from each of five scanners for every region: AT2, GT450, DP200, P1000, and B300 \citep{ryu2025scorpion}. The resulting 480 regions and 2,400 patches are real human histopathology images.

The original slide is the split, bootstrap, and permutation unit. All ten regions and all five scanner views from a slide remain in the same partition. Patch-level or region-level random splitting would expose nearly identical tissue content across train and test sets and is therefore prohibited.

\subsection{Frozen representation audit}
The first audit used three unrelated representation families: ImageNet-pretrained ResNet50 \citep{he2016deep}, general-purpose self-supervised DINOv2-Base \citep{oquab2023dinov2}, and pathology-native Phikon \citep{filiot2023scaling}. For each frozen feature space, a logistic scanner probe was trained on slide-disjoint training data and evaluated on held-out slides. Tissue identity was measured by bidirectional cross-scanner region retrieval.

Scanner identity is therefore not a ResNet-specific texture artifact. It remains strongly recoverable from a general self-supervised vision transformer and is strongest in the pathology-native encoder, even while tissue identity is preserved. DINOv2-Base was selected as the development substrate because it provided nearly saturated tissue retrieval together with substantial residual scanner information.

\section{PathoAlign paired-factor model}
Let $x_{r,s}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$ denote the frozen feature for tissue region $r$ scanned by scanner $s$. PathoAlign learns
\begin{equation}
  z_b=f_b(x_{r,s})\in\mathbb{R}^{256},
  \qquad
  z_a=f_a(x_{r,s})\in\mathbb{R}^{64},
\end{equation}
where $z_b$ is the biological representation and $z_a$ is the acquisition representation. A decoder reconstructs the standardized frozen feature:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{x}_{r,s}=g([z_b,z_a]).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Paired biological objective}
Every contrastive minibatch contains all five scanner views of each selected tissue region. The biological branch uses a multi-positive supervised contrastive loss in which all other scanner views of the same region are positives and views of different regions are negatives:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pair}}=
  -\frac{1}{B}\sum_i
  \frac{1}{|P(i)|}
  \sum_{p\in P(i)}
  \log
  \frac{\exp(\operatorname{sim}(z_{b,i},z_{b,p})/\tau)}
  {\sum_{j\neq i}\exp(\operatorname{sim}(z_{b,i},z_{b,j})/\tau)}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Direct scanner-dependence control}
Gradient reversal alone was insufficient in an initial smoke experiment. The frozen objective therefore adds a direct normalized linear-dependence penalty between biological features and one-hot scanner labels. Let $\widetilde Z_b$ and $\widetilde Y_s$ be column-standardized batch matrices. Then
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dep}}
  =\left\|
  \frac{1}{B-1}\widetilde Z_b^{\top}\widetilde Y_s
  \right\|_F^2.
\end{equation}
This targets the same class of linear dependence measured by the held-out scanner probe without forcing the biological representation to become constant.

\subsection{Acquisition retention and factor separation}
The acquisition branch is trained to predict scanner identity with cross-entropy $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{scan},a}$. A scanner classifier connected through gradient reversal discourages scanner prediction from $z_b$, producing $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{scan},b}$. Cross-covariance penalizes redundant information between the two factors:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{xcov}}
  =\left\|
  \frac{1}{B-1}
  (Z_b-\overline Z_b)^{\top}(Z_a-\overline Z_a)
  \right\|_F^2.
\end{equation}
Reconstruction, variance, and within-branch covariance terms prevent collapse and retain information needed to represent the input.

The frozen total objective is
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}
  &=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pair}}
  +\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{recon}}
  +\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{var}}
  +0.01\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cov}} \\
  &\quad+0.5\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{scan},b}
  +0.5\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{scan},a}
  +20\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dep}}
  +0.05\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{xcov}}.
\end{align}
The comparison method uses the same biological projection, paired contrastive loss, reconstruction, variance, and covariance terms, but no acquisition branch or scanner-separation losses.

\section{Frozen development and evaluation protocol}
The study separated objective selection from confirmation and final fold evaluation.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Bounded calibration.} Four predefined PathoAlign dependence settings were compared with one fixed paired-consistency reference using seeds 401--403 on fold-0 train/validation data. The dependence weight 20 setting was selected because it reduced scanner leakage while preserving tissue retrieval.
  \item \textbf{Independent-seed confirmation.} The selected objective was frozen and tested with new seeds 501--510 on the same fold-0 train/validation partition. PathoAlign reduced scanner-probe balanced accuracy by 0.3958 relative to paired consistency, with all ten seeds favorable and exact sign-flip $p=0.001953$.
  \item \textbf{Five-fold test stage.} No objective, architecture, schedule, or threshold was changed. For each rotating fold, all non-test slides were used for fitting because model selection was complete. Seeds 601--605 were new. Every original slide served as test exactly once per seed and method.
\end{enumerate}

Each model used 75 epochs, region batch size 32, AdamW learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, and weight decay $10^{-4}$. Inferential contrasts average optimization seeds within slide and use the 48 original slides as independent blocks.

The scanner-probe and paired-cosine Monte Carlo sign-flip tests each gave $p=0.000004$ with 250,000 draws. Retrieval differences were statistically and practically negligible. Their lower confidence bounds are far above the predefined non-inferiority margin of $-0.02$. Most slides were exact retrieval ties at or near perfect accuracy, so the low count of strictly positive retrieval differences does not indicate meaningful loss.

\subsection{Factorization audit}
Across the test folds, the acquisition branch achieved 0.8602 scanner-probe accuracy, 0.1042 mean tissue retrieval, effective rank 5.44, and normalized biological/acquisition cross-covariance RMS 0.0928. Every biological dimension retained nonzero test-fold variance. Biological effective rank decreased modestly from 56.93 to 54.50 rather than collapsing.

These diagnostics distinguish separation from erasure. Scanner information remains available in a compact acquisition factor, tissue identity remains nearly saturated in biology, and biological scanner leakage is substantially lower. The result is strong but not perfect disentanglement: residual scanner information remains in $z_b$, and residual tissue information remains in $z_a$.

\section{Supporting identifiability studies}
\subsection{Negative results before paired real-tissue factorization}
Earlier PathoAlign experiments used adversarial center removal and factorized diagnostic/site branches on pathology-derived representations. A locked adversarial confirmation reduced site leakage only slightly and did not improve held-out tumor accuracy. Unconditional subtraction was falsified because the learned acquisition-associated component remained strongly tumor-predictive. These results motivate the present design: acquisition information should be isolated under biology-preserving pairing, not removed solely because it predicts site.

\subsection{Synthetic recovery regimes}
Controlled generators with known biological and acquisition factors varied observational sample size, paired-anchor count, repeated exposure, biological--acquisition overlap, linear versus nonlinear mixing, method, and seed. The resulting phase maps show that paired interventions and ordinary observations are distinct resources. Recovery becomes harder as overlap and nonlinearity increase, and matched-budget experiments show that total paired exposure is the dominant resource over the tested range, with a smaller benefit from anchor diversity and diminishing returns beyond approximately 100 unique anchors.

The detailed calculations and threshold-dependent empirical recovery models are included below as a supporting synthetic extension. They are not used to claim a universal scaling law or clinical performance.

\section{Secondary federated influence stress test}
A separate PANDA-derived experiment studies model influence rather than representation factorization. Standard FedAvg weights simulated clients by sample count \citep{mcmahan2017communication}. Controlled dominant-client label-noise and ordinal-threshold shifts show that sample count can grant excessive influence to a training process that is poorly aligned with the declared validation objective. A fixed detector-switch rule calibrated under label noise transferred to stronger conservative threshold shifts with low clean-regime switching and positive improvements in global QWK, macro-F1, and worst-site QWK.

This result is deliberately secondary in the present paper. It does not rank institutions or claim that large sites are untrustworthy. It demonstrates that aggregation influence, like representation removal, should be audited rather than treated as self-justifying.

\section{Limitations}
The central result is limited to the SCORPION benchmark, its 48 original slides, five scanners, sampled tissue regions, and frozen DINOv2 features. It does not establish generalization to new tissue types, laboratories, staining protocols, scanner models outside the benchmark, or end-to-end whole-slide diagnostic systems. Cross-backbone transfer of the frozen objective to Phikon and ResNet50 remains an important next test.

Tissue-region retrieval is an identity-retention diagnostic, not a clinical endpoint. The study does not evaluate diagnosis, treatment decisions, patient outcomes, prospective deployment, subgroup fairness, regulatory readiness, or scanner equivalence for clinical use. Linear probes measure recoverable scanner information under a specified model class; they do not prove that all scanner information has been removed. The acquisition factor remains imperfect because it retains some tissue identity, and the biological factor remains imperfect because scanner prediction is above chance.

The synthetic recovery equations are local empirical descriptions of tested thresholds and regimes. The federated experiments use simulated clients over fixed pathology-derived features rather than prospective institutional federation. All claims are therefore bounded to representation separation and controlled stress testing.

\section{Discussion}
The multi-backbone audit demonstrates that strong tissue representations can simultaneously preserve scanner identity. Domain-specific pretraining does not automatically solve acquisition entanglement: Phikon had the strongest scanner probe of the three frozen encoders. DINOv2 provided a particularly clean test because tissue retrieval was already nearly perfect, leaving little room for a method to claim success by simply improving identity retention.

The five-fold result shows that paired consistency alone is not the complete solution. It preserves tissue identity and reduces some scanner leakage, but the factorized PathoAlign objective produces a much larger reduction while improving paired agreement. The acquisition audit matters as much as the biological probe: without it, lower scanner accuracy in biology could be caused by information destruction. Here, scanner identity remains strongly available in a low-rank acquisition factor, retrieval remains saturated in biology, and all biological dimensions remain active.

The broader lesson is not that scanner information is always useless. Acquisition and biology may overlap, and some acquisition-correlated directions may carry task-relevant morphology. The role of paired interventions is to provide controlled evidence about what should remain stable. Future work should transfer the frozen objective unchanged across backbones, evaluate task-labeled paired-scanner datasets, extend the method to patch bags and multiple-instance learning, and test unseen laboratories and acquisition pipelines.

\section{Conclusion}
PathoAlign reframes scanner robustness as a neural identifiability problem. On real paired human histopathology, scanner identity was strongly recoverable from ResNet50, DINOv2, and Phikon representations despite high tissue retention. A frozen factorized objective then generalized across all 48 SCORPION slides, reducing biological scanner-probe accuracy by approximately 0.385 absolute, improving same-tissue scanner-pair agreement by approximately 0.031, and preserving near-perfect tissue retrieval. Scanner information remained concentrated in a compact acquisition factor, and the biological representation did not collapse.

The supported conclusion is narrow but substantive: biology-preserving paired acquisitions can supervise a representation in which tissue identity is retained while scanner information becomes substantially less recoverable. This is a real-tissue out-of-fold representation result, not a claim of clinical validation or universal disentanglement.
