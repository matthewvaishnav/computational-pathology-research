\begin{abstract}
Reliable federated oncology requires more than a private communication channel or a strong slide classifier. It requires auditing three coupled levels of learning: what information is encoded in a pathology representation, how patch evidence is aggregated into a slide prediction, and how institutional influence is assigned during federated training. This technical report organizes one research platform around three distinct components. PathoAlign is a neural representation-alignment program that uses biology-preserving paired acquisitions to separate tissue identity from acquisition signal. TransnnMIL is a custom multiple-instance learning model that combines a transformer-based correlated branch with an nnMIL-style gated-attention branch for whole-slide inference. PathologyFL is a custom federated-learning framework containing FedAvg baselines, contribution-aware weighting, FAIR-WEIGHTS-H mechanisms, and dominance-aware switching.

The strongest PathoAlign result uses 2,400 real human histopathology patches from the SCORPION paired-scanner benchmark. Relative to paired consistency alone, a frozen five-fold PathoAlign objective reduced biological scanner-probe accuracy from 0.7825 to 0.3989 while improving paired agreement by approximately 0.031 and preserving cross-scanner tissue retrieval above 0.9987. TransnnMIL has been evaluated for optimization stability on 10,611 readable PANDA Phikon feature bags: across 18 runs spanning six learning rates and three seeds, mean best validation quadratic weighted kappa ranged from 0.8117 to 0.8257. This supports stability under a careful recipe, not yet superiority over simpler MIL baselines. PathologyFL experiments show that standard sample-weighted FedAvg remains competitive in clean conditions but can become vulnerable when sample volume and client reliability diverge. On the full PANDA feature cache across 15 seeds, a tuned observable switch preserved clean performance while improving global QWK at 25\% and 35\% dominant-site corruption. Camelyon17 external-center studies additionally found held-out-center accuracy of 0.9052 for FedAvg-style weighting versus 0.9318 and 0.9322 for equal-client and dominant-center-downweighting policies.

The platform is privacy-aware because PathologyFL is designed to keep raw pathology data local and exposes privacy-engine, secure-aggregation, and differential-privacy interfaces. The present experiments do not establish a formal privacy guarantee, clinical validity, or end-to-end hospital deployment. The supported conclusion is narrower: representation alignment, slide aggregation, and institutional aggregation are separate failure surfaces, and each requires explicit, reproducible audits before the complete pipeline can be trusted.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Computational pathology systems learn across a hierarchy: local image patches are encoded into representations, patch evidence is aggregated into slide- or patient-level predictions, and multi-institutional training combines information across sites. Failure at any one level can be hidden by a strong aggregate score.

At the representation level, scanner, staining, preparation, institution, and tissue morphology can be statistically entangled. Removing site information indiscriminately can erase useful biology. At the whole-slide level, thousands of correlated patch embeddings must be aggregated under weak supervision. At the federation level, sample-weighted FedAvg silently assumes that more local samples should grant more authority over the global model \citep{mcmahan2017communication}. That assumption can fail when sample volume, label policy, acquisition workflow, and task-relevant signal diverge.

This report separates these questions into three named components:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{PathoAlign} addresses neural representation alignment and identifiability.
  \item \textbf{TransnnMIL} addresses whole-slide multiple-instance learning.
  \item \textbf{PathologyFL} addresses privacy-aware federated training and institutional aggregation.
\end{itemize}

FAIR-WEIGHTS-H, cross-site contribution-aware weighting, and dominance-aware detector switching are methods within PathologyFL; they are not separate umbrella systems. The complete platform is therefore broader than any one component.

\subsection*{Working thesis}
Reliable federated oncology requires auditable control over what information is retained in representations, how local tissue evidence is combined into a prediction, and how much influence each institution receives. Improvement at one level does not certify the others.

\section{Contributions}
The report makes four top-level contributions.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{PathoAlign: biology-preserving representation alignment.} A real-tissue multi-backbone audit, paired-acquisition factorization objective, locked five-fold SCORPION result, factor-retention audit, negative results, and matched-budget synthetic identifiability studies.
  \item \textbf{TransnnMIL: custom whole-slide learning.} A dual-branch fusion of TransMIL-style correlated modeling and nnMIL-style gated aggregation, with optional hierarchical and topology extensions, together with repeated-seed PANDA optimization-stability evidence.

\section{Component I: PathoAlign neural representation alignment}
\subsection{Real paired-scanner benchmark}
The SCORPION release contains 48 original H\&E slides, ten aligned regions per slide, and one image from each of five scanners for every region: AT2, GT450, DP200, P1000, and B300 \citep{ryu2025scorpion}. The resulting 480 regions and 2,400 patches are real human histopathology images.

The original slide is the split, bootstrap, and permutation unit. All ten regions and all five scanner views from a slide remain in the same partition. Patch-level or region-level random splitting would expose nearly identical tissue content across train and test sets and is therefore prohibited.

\subsection{Frozen representation audit}
The audit used ImageNet-pretrained ResNet50 \citep{he2016deep}, general-purpose self-supervised DINOv2-Base \citep{oquab2023dinov2}, and pathology-native Phikon \citep{filiot2023scaling}. A logistic scanner probe was trained on slide-disjoint training data and evaluated on held-out slides. Tissue identity was measured by bidirectional cross-scanner region retrieval.

Scanner identity is therefore not confined to one encoder family. It remains strongly recoverable while tissue identity is preserved. DINOv2-Base was selected as the development substrate because tissue retrieval was nearly saturated while residual scanner information remained substantial.

\subsection{Paired-factor model}
Let $x_{r,s}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$ denote the frozen feature for tissue region $r$ scanned by scanner $s$. PathoAlign learns
\begin{equation}
  z_b=f_b(x_{r,s})\in\mathbb{R}^{256},
  \qquad
  z_a=f_a(x_{r,s})\in\mathbb{R}^{64},
\end{equation}
where $z_b$ is the biological representation and $z_a$ is the acquisition representation. A decoder reconstructs the standardized frozen feature:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{x}_{r,s}=g([z_b,z_a]).
\end{equation}

Every contrastive minibatch contains all five scanner views of each selected tissue region. The biological branch uses a multi-positive supervised contrastive loss in which other scanner views of the same region are positives and different regions are negatives:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pair}}=
  -\frac{1}{B}\sum_i
  \frac{1}{|P(i)|}
  \sum_{p\in P(i)}
  \log
  \frac{\exp(\operatorname{sim}(z_{b,i},z_{b,p})/\tau)}
  {\sum_{j\neq i}\exp(\operatorname{sim}(z_{b,i},z_{b,j})/\tau)}.
\end{equation}

Gradient reversal alone was insufficient in an initial smoke experiment. The frozen objective therefore adds a direct normalized linear-dependence penalty between biological features and one-hot scanner labels. Let $\widetilde Z_b$ and $\widetilde Y_s$ be column-standardized batch matrices:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dep}}
  =\left\|
  \frac{1}{B-1}\widetilde Z_b^{\top}\widetilde Y_s
  \right\|_F^2.
\end{equation}

The acquisition branch predicts scanner identity. A gradient-reversal scanner classifier discourages scanner prediction from $z_b$. Cross-covariance penalizes redundant information between factors:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{xcov}}
  =\left\|
  \frac{1}{B-1}
  (Z_b-\overline Z_b)^{\top}(Z_a-\overline Z_a)
  \right\|_F^2.
\end{equation}
Reconstruction, variance, and within-branch covariance terms prevent collapse.

The frozen total objective is
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}
  &=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pair}}
  +\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{recon}}
  +\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{var}}
  +0.01\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cov}} \\
  &\quad+0.5\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{scan},b}
  +0.5\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{scan},a}
  +20\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dep}}
  +0.05\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{xcov}}.
\end{align}

\subsection{Frozen development and evaluation protocol}
The study separated objective selection from confirmation and final fold evaluation.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Bounded calibration.} Four predefined dependence settings were compared with one fixed paired-consistency reference using seeds 401--403 on fold-0 train/validation data.
  \item \textbf{Independent-seed confirmation.} The selected objective was frozen and tested with new seeds 501--510. PathoAlign reduced scanner-probe balanced accuracy by 0.3958 relative to paired consistency, with all ten seeds favorable and exact sign-flip $p=0.001953$.
  \item \textbf{Five-fold test stage.} No objective, architecture, schedule, or threshold was changed. Each original slide served as test exactly once per seed and method.
\end{enumerate}
Each model used 75 epochs, region batch size 32, AdamW learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, and weight decay $10^{-4}$. Inferential contrasts average optimization seeds within slide and use the 48 original slides as independent blocks.

These diagnostics distinguish separation from erasure. Scanner information remains available in a compact acquisition factor, tissue identity remains nearly saturated in biology, and biological scanner leakage is substantially lower. The result is strong but not perfect disentanglement.

\subsection{Supporting identifiability studies}
Earlier adversarial center-removal and factorized diagnostic/site experiments reduced site leakage only slightly and did not improve held-out tumor accuracy. Unconditional subtraction was falsified because the learned acquisition-associated component remained strongly tumor-predictive. These negative results motivate the paired design: acquisition information should be isolated under biology-preserving interventions, not removed solely because it predicts site.

Controlled synthetic generators varied observational sample size, paired-anchor count, repeated exposure, biological--acquisition overlap, nonlinear mixing, method, and seed. Matched-budget experiments identify total paired exposure as the dominant resource over the tested range, with a smaller benefit from anchor diversity and diminishing returns beyond approximately 100 unique anchors. Detailed calculations are retained in the appendix as a supporting synthetic extension, not as a universal scaling law.

\section{Component II: TransnnMIL whole-slide modeling}
\subsection{Model definition}
A whole-slide image is represented as a bag of patch embeddings $B_i=\{x_{i1},\ldots,x_{im_i}\}$ with one slide- or patient-level label. TransnnMIL is the repository's custom slide learner. Its validated baseline implementation combines two parallel branches:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item a TransMIL branch for correlated patch interactions through transformer self-attention \citep{shao2021transmil};
  \item an nnMIL-style gated-attention branch for lightweight stochastic aggregation \citep{luo2025nnmil}.
\end{itemize}
Both branches process the same bag. Their intermediate features are projected to a common 512-dimensional space, fused by cross-attention, and mapped to class logits. Positional encoding can be disabled for compatibility with random sub-bag sampling. The code additionally exposes optional hierarchical spatial pooling and a topology branch, but those extensions are not treated as validated improvements in this report.

The component addresses a different problem from PathoAlign. PathoAlign changes the representation supplied to a learner; TransnnMIL determines how evidence distributed across a slide is combined into a prediction.

\subsection{PANDA optimization-stability evidence}
The completed stability grid used 10,611 readable PANDA Phikon feature bags, a 768-dimensional feature space, three seeds, and six learning rates. The stabilized recipe used AdamW, warmup-cosine scheduling, two warmup epochs, gradient clipping at norm 1.0, and early stopping.

\subsection{Required architecture ablation}
A decisive evaluation should control the feature extractor, patient split, patch-presentation budget, optimizer, scheduler, stopping rule, and seeds while varying:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item TransMIL branch on/off;
  \item nnMIL branch on/off;
  \item learned fusion versus fixed averaging;
  \item random patch sampling on/off;
  \item random feature sampling on/off;
  \item positional or hierarchical structure on/off;
  \item sliding-window ensembling on/off.
\end{enumerate}
External-center performance, calibration, patch-sampling variance, and computational cost should be reported alongside QWK or AUC.

\section{Component III: PathologyFL federated training}
\subsection{Framework scope}
PathologyFL is the custom federated-learning framework in the repository. It separates client-side local training from coordinator-side aggregation, registry, monitoring, privacy, and robustness services. Its aggregation layer includes standard FedAvg, contribution-aware cross-site weighting, FAIR-WEIGHTS-H mechanisms, and dominance-aware policy switching.

For client updates $\Delta\theta_k$, the standard and dominance-aware candidates are
\[
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{FA}}
=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_k}{\sum_{j=1}^{K}n_j}\Delta\theta_k,
\qquad
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{DA}}
=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\widetilde w_k\Delta\theta_k.
\]
A clean-calibrated diagnostic vector
\[
d(r)=\bigl(\kappa_{\mathrm{global}},\kappa_{\min},S_\kappa,
\operatorname{MAOE},R_{\mathrm{sev}}\bigr)
\]
induces a trigger count $C(r)=\sum_{j=1}^{5}I_j(r)$ and switch $T(r)=\mathbf{1}[C(r)\geq3]$. The selected policy is
\[
M_{\pi}(r)=[1-T(r)]M_{\mathrm{FA}}(r)+T(r)M_{\mathrm{DA}}(r).
\]

\subsection{Full-PANDA dominance-aware result}
The strongest controlled result uses 10,611 readable PANDA Phikon feature vectors, five simulated sites, 15 seeds, and label corruption applied only to the largest simulated site. The clean strategy is FedAvg and the fallback is a 50\% cross-site blend.

\subsection{Privacy and communication boundary}
PathologyFL is privacy-aware by architecture: raw pathology data remains local, and the framework exposes secure-aggregation and differential-privacy support surfaces. However, local data retention alone is not a formal privacy guarantee. The current privacy evidence is a coefficient-noise robustness probe, not differential-privacy accounting, membership-inference evaluation, update-inversion resistance, or verified secure aggregation.

Communication has a bounded feature/head result. Under one explicit Camelyon17 accounting regime, 100 fp32 rounds across three source clients require approximately 24.98 GB for full ResNet18 model exchange versus 2.35 MB for a 512-to-2 feature/head learner. This demonstrates a practical low-communication regime, not a universal solution to full-model federated pathology.

\section{Limitations}
PathoAlign is currently strongest as a representation-factorization result on SCORPION's 48 original slides, five scanners, sampled tissue regions, and frozen features. Tissue retrieval is an identity-retention diagnostic, not a clinical endpoint. Linear probes measure recoverable scanner information under a specified model class; they do not prove removal of all acquisition information.

TransnnMIL's completed evidence is primarily optimization stability. The architecture still requires controlled component ablations, external cohorts, calibration analysis, and direct comparison against TransMIL, nnMIL, AttentionMIL, and simple pooling under equal compute.

PathologyFL uses simulated clients over fixed pathology-derived features for its strongest controlled stress tests. Camelyon17 provides natural external-center evidence but remains feature-level. The project has not yet demonstrated formal differential privacy, a verified secure-aggregation deployment, leakage resistance, prospective hospital federation, subgroup clinical safety, or regulatory readiness.

The integrated stack has not been executed as one locked experiment. No result in one component should be used to imply that the other two components have been validated.

\section{Discussion}
The three components expose related but non-identical forms of hidden authority. In a representation, a nuisance-correlated direction can dominate what a downstream model learns. In a slide learner, a small subset of patches or one aggregation branch can dominate the prediction. In federation, a high-volume client can dominate the global update. Each authority mechanism can be reasonable in one regime and harmful in another.

PathoAlign shows that lower site leakage is not sufficient; useful biology must be retained and the removed information must be accounted for. TransnnMIL shows that whole-slide model quality cannot be inferred from one tuned run; optimizer stability and branch-level ablation are necessary. PathologyFL shows that FedAvg is not universally weak, but its sample-volume rule should be stress-tested when reliability and volume may diverge.

The platform's value is therefore not a claim that one large architecture solves computational pathology. It is a decomposition of the reliability problem into auditable levels with explicit interfaces, baselines, negative results, and next experiments.

\section{Conclusion}
This report defines a computational pathology research platform for federated oncology with three separate components. PathoAlign addresses biology-preserving neural representation alignment, TransnnMIL addresses whole-slide evidence aggregation, and PathologyFL addresses privacy-aware federated training and institutional influence. The strongest current results are a frozen real-tissue paired-scanner PathoAlign separation result, a repeated-seed TransnnMIL stability result, and PathologyFL evidence that dominance-aware switching can preserve FedAvg in clean regimes while improving controlled stress and external-center performance.

The supported conclusion is deliberately bounded: reliable federated oncology requires separate audits of representation, slide aggregation, and federation. The complete integrated pipeline remains a testable research program rather than a finished clinical system.
