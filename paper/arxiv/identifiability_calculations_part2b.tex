\subsection{Exact anchor diversity--repetition pilot}
A subsequent exact-schedule pilot separated unique paired anchors $N_p$ from presentations per anchor $R_p$. Every selected anchor appeared exactly $R_p$ times in a deterministic shuffled schedule. The pilot crossed $N_u\in\{750,1500\}$, $N_p\in\{50,125,225\}$, $R_p\in\{32,64,128\}$, two methods, and five new seeds, for 180 neural-network fits.

Increasing repetition improved the universal biological score in all 12 fixed-$(N_u,N_p,\text{method})$ slices. Increasing unique-anchor diversity also improved it in all 12 fixed-$(N_u,R_p,\text{method})$ slices.

Task AUC remained approximately $0.96$--$0.98$ while biological recovery, factor separation, and paired-invariance error changed substantially. Strong task prediction therefore did not certify a disentangled biological representation.

\subsection{Matched paired-supervision budget}
To isolate intervention diversity from repeated exposure, a matched-budget experiment fixed total paired presentations at
\begin{equation}
  N_pR_p=6400,
\end{equation}
and fixed pair-loss updates at 100. It compared $50\times128$, $100\times64$, and $200\times32$ allocations across $N_u\in\{750,1500\}$, hybrid curriculum and pair consistency, and 10 matched seeds, for 120 fits.

\midrule
$50\times128$ & 0.4081 & ---\\
$100\times64$ & 0.4243 & 0.0163\\
$200\times32$ & 0.4259 & 0.0179\\
\bottomrule

Both higher-diversity allocations exceeded $50\times128$ in mean recovery in all four strata. The $100\times64$ allocation improved the score by 0.0163 on average, and $200\times32$ improved it by 0.0179. Increasing from 100 to 200 unique anchors added only 0.0016 on average and had inconsistent stratum-level effects, indicating diminishing returns beyond approximately 100 anchors in this tested regime.

Paired seed-level contrasts supported a cautious interpretation. The strongest individual contrast was hybrid curriculum at $N_u=750$, where $100\times64$ exceeded $50\times128$ by 0.0226 with a bootstrap interval of $[0.0067,0.0387]$ and an exact two-sided sign-flip $p$-value of 0.029. The other stratum-specific intervals generally included zero, and no individual test is treated as confirmatory after accounting for multiple comparisons.

The matched-budget result therefore provides consistent descriptive evidence that allocating supervision across more unique paired interventions is preferable to excessive rehearsal of a small anchor set. It also shows that total paired presentations alone are insufficient to describe supervision value: the allocation between unique-anchor diversity and repetition matters. None of the 6400-presentation conditions crossed the predefined recovery threshold of 0.50, so this experiment identifies resource-allocation efficiency rather than a recovery boundary.
